1. Interactive Canvas-First Drawing Tools

Quick Toolbar

The primary drawing workflow takes place directly on the 600×580 canvas viewport using interactive rubberbanding. As you sketch, coordinates, angles, and lengths are calculated in real time, and the sidebar forms automatically synchronize for fine-tuning.

Select & Edit Tool V / S

Click any element on the canvas to inspect its parameters. Drag shape endpoints or control handles to interactively reposition them.

Straight Line Tool L

Click and drag to span a straight line segment. Supports arrowheads (->, <-, <->), thicknesses (thin to ultra thick), dash styles, and real-time angle/length telemetry.

4-Point Bézier Curve C

Construct cubic Bézier splines by placing 4 points: Start point ($P_1$), End point ($P_2$), Control point 1 ($C_1$), and Control point 2 ($C_2$). Includes an arc shortcut (Match C₂ = C₁) and step-by-step undo.

Rectangle & Area Tool R

Rubberband drag to shade economic profit boxes, consumer surplus zones, or deadweight loss regions with customizable solid or dashed borders and fills.

Circle Tool O

Drag from center to radius or bounding box to draw circles, indifference bliss points, or unit circles with center point coordinates and optional fills.

Point & Marker Tool P

Click anywhere on the canvas to place labeled points ($A, B, E_1, \text{Max}$). Select from 8 cardinal label anchor positions (above right, below left, etc.).

Pro Tip — Floating Confirmation Popover: When drawing with Confirm Mode enabled, a compact on-canvas card displays real-time dimensions, length, slope angle, and allows you to rename labels, set line style (Solid/Dashed), pick colors, or press Enter to confirm or Esc to discard.

2. Curated Domain Model Presets (14 Ready-to-Use Templates)

Templates

TikZ Studio includes 14 pre-built academic figures across economics, mathematics, and physics. Select any model from the Model dropdown in the toolbar or click All Models → in the sidebar to populate the canvas:

Microeconomics
  • Supply & Demand: Equilibrium $E^*$, consumer and producer surplus lines.
  • Monopoly & DWL: Downward demand, marginal revenue (MR), marginal cost (MC), deadweight loss area.
  • Utility Maximization: Convex indifference curve tangent to budget constraint.
  • Firm Cost Curves: U-shaped MC, ATC, AVC intersecting at minimum ATC.
  • Production Possibility (PPF): Concave frontier showing resource allocation trade-offs.
  • Negative Externality: Private vs. Social Marginal Cost with Pigouvian tax gap.
Macroeconomics
  • IS-LM Macro Model: Downward-sloping IS and upward-sloping LM intersecting at interest rate $r^*$ and output $Y^*$.
  • Keynesian Cross (45°): Aggregate expenditure curve $AE$ intersecting 45-degree guideline.
  • Solow-Swan Growth: Capital accumulation, saving curve $s \cdot f(k)$, and depreciation line $(n+g+\delta)k$.
  • AD-AS Equilibrium: Aggregate demand, short-run AS, and vertical long-run aggregate supply.
Mathematics, Stats & Physics
  • Normal Distribution: Symmetric Gaussian bell curve with $\mu$ and standard deviation markers.
  • Trigonometric Unit Circle: Circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with reference angle $\theta$, $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$.
  • Lorenz Curve & Gini: 45° equality benchmark and convex income distribution curve.
  • Projectile Motion: Parabolic trajectory showing initial velocity vector $v_0$ and launch angle $\theta$.

3. Canvas Layers & Z-Index Management

Alt + L

In LaTeX TikZ, elements are drawn in code sequential order — shapes declared earlier appear underneath shapes declared later. The Canvas Layers slide-out drawer provides complete control over this visual hierarchy:

Front-to-Back Ordering

Drag layer items up or down to reorder the Z-index. Place background shaded rectangles at the bottom and key labels on top.

Lock to Protect

Click the lock icon on completed curves or axes to prevent accidental dragging, clicks, or coordinate shifts while drawing nearby items.

Visibility Toggle & Search

Temporarily hide dense annotations with the eye icon, or use the search bar and category pills (Lines, Curves, Shapes, Points) to isolate items quickly.

6. Compiling in LaTeX & Overleaf

The generated code is 100% standard PGF/TikZ and compiles with pdfLaTeX, LuaLaTeX, or XeLaTeX without external dependencies:

\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{tikz}
\begin{document}

% Paste the generated TikZ code from TikZ Studio here:
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.8]
  % Axes, lines, curves, rectangles, and labels
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (10,0) node[right] {$Quantity$};
  \draw[->, thick] (0,0) -- (0,10) node[above] {$Price$};
\end{tikzpicture}

\end{document}
